Weddings

Entertainment for every wedding function

An Indian wedding is many events, and each one calls for different music. Pick the function you are planning to see the kinds of acts that suit it, and the artists you can book.

Pre-wedding celebrations

Families often book different artists for each of these.

  • Engagement / Ring ceremony (Sagai)An engagement is intimate and romantic, so the music is usually soft, melodic and easy to talk over.
  • Haldi & Mehendi ceremonyHaldi and mehendi are the relaxed, colourful days before the wedding: guests sit, play and sing for hours.
  • Sangeet & Cocktail nightThe sangeet and the cocktail evening are the big entertainment nights of a wedding, with performances.
  • Pool partyDaytime or sunset by the water: the music is upbeat, rhythmic and relaxed.
  • Jaggo / Ladies sangeetA traditional, high-energy night of folk songs, dhol and dancing, usually led by the women of the family.
  • Welcome dinner / Family dinnerA dinner for guests arriving from out of town: the music should set the mood without getting in the way of.

Wedding-day ceremonies

Separate bookings, even when they happen on the same day.

  • Baraat / Ghodi entryThe groom's arrival is loud and joyous: the music has to lead the procession and make the whole street dance.
  • Varmala / Jaimala ceremonyA short, emotional moment where the couple exchange garlands, usually with soft live music in the background.
  • Wedding ceremony / PherasThe pheras are sacred, so the music is traditional and respectful, played softly around the rituals.
  • VidaaiThe farewell is the most emotional moment of the day, and the music should hold that feeling.

Post-wedding celebrations

The reception and the parties around it.

  • Wedding receptionThe reception is a formal, glamorous evening for a large guest list, so the music has to suit every age.
  • After partyThe late-night party after the formal events: loud, fast and made for the younger crowd.
  • Post-wedding brunch / Farewell lunchA relaxed last gathering, where light music sets a warm, unhurried mood.

Other functions and celebrations

Ceremonies, special evenings and gatherings around the wedding.

  • Roka ceremonyA small, early family ceremony where soft, romantic music is enough.
  • Chunni / Chunri ceremonyA traditional ceremony of blessings, usually accompanied by folk or devotional singing.
  • Chooda ceremonyA Punjabi ceremony before the wedding, with folk songs and dhol.
  • Mayra / BhaatA Rajasthani and North Indian tradition where the maternal family blesses the couple, often with folk singing.
  • Mata ki ChowkiA devotional evening of bhajans and jagrata, often held before the wedding functions begin.
  • Jagran / JagrataAn all-night devotional gathering of bhajans and prayers.
  • Qawwali nightA powerful, devotional-turned-celebratory evening of qawwali, loved by guests of all ages.
  • Sufi nightAn evening of Sufi music, soulful and often paired with dinner or a cocktail hour.
  • Ghazal eveningA refined, seated evening of ghazals, usually for family and older guests.
  • Bollywood retro nightA nostalgia night of classic Bollywood hits that gets every generation singing.
  • Musical eveningA relaxed programme of live music, usually before dinner, for family and close friends.
  • Bachelor / Bachelorette partyA party for the couple's friends, usually a late, high-energy night.
  • Family get-togetherA relaxed gathering of both families where the music should be gentle and inclusive.
  • Destination wedding welcomeGuests arriving at a destination wedding are welcomed with local flavour, so folk and cultural acts work well.
