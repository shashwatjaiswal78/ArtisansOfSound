Booking Agent or Artist Manager: Who Do You Actually Need?

14 November 2026 | 3 min read

Looking to book an act? We handle the quote, the contract and the logistics for you.

People in the music industry use the words booking and management interchangeably, but they describe different jobs. If you are planning an event, or if you are an artist deciding who should represent you, the difference matters.

Artist booking

Booking is about a single event. A booking agent or company connects a client with an artist for a specific date, handles the quote, confirms the price and timing, and makes sure the artist turns up and performs. The relationship is usually transactional. Once the event is over, the job is done.

Artist management

Management is about the artist's career. A manager works for the artist over months and years. That means finding the right opportunities, negotiating fees, handling contracts, planning travel, protecting the artist's time and helping them grow their audience and reputation. A good manager thinks about where the artist should be in two years, not only next Saturday.

Where the two overlap

A company like ours does both. We manage artists, which means we represent them, look after their schedules and make sure they are paid fairly and on time. We also book them for clients, which means we match the right act to your venue, crowd and budget. Because we manage the artists we book, we know them well, we vouch for them, and we can tell you honestly whether an act suits your event.

Why this matters to a client

  • An act with proper management turns up prepared, with clear terms and a contract.
  • You deal with one point of contact instead of chasing individual artists.
  • If something changes, there is someone accountable who can solve it.

Why this matters to an artist

Playing well is only half the work. Chasing enquiries, negotiating, following up on payments and handling logistics takes time away from the music. The right management takes that load off, so the artist can focus on the craft.

Which one do you need?

If you are a client planning a wedding, party or corporate event, you need booking: someone who can match you with the right act, confirm the details and make sure the performance happens as agreed. If you are an artist, you may need management: someone who handles enquiries, negotiations, contracts and payments while you work on your craft.

Questions to ask a company that offers both

  • Do you personally know the artists you book, or do you only pass on enquiries?
  • Is every quote itemised, with the fee, duration, travel and hospitality clearly stated?
  • Is there a written contract, and what does it say about cancellation and rescheduling?
  • Who is my single point of contact if something changes before the event?

A company that also manages artists has a stake in their reputation. It will not send an unsuitable act to your event, because that damages both sides. Artisans of Sound was founded by an artist, and we are based in Delhi and Lucknow, so we see both sides of every booking.

Frequently asked questions

Is a booking agent the same as a manager?

No. A booking agent arranges individual performances. A manager looks after an artist's wider career, including opportunities, contracts and long term planning.

Do artists need both?

Most working artists need someone to handle bookings and someone to look after the bigger picture. At smaller scale, one company can do both, as we do.

How does a management company earn its money?

Arrangements differ between companies, and some work on commission or an agreed fee. Whoever you work with, ask for the terms in writing before you sign.

Can I book an artist directly instead of going through a company?

You can, but you then handle the quote, the contract, the logistics and any problems yourself. A company takes that load on and stands behind the booking.

What should a management agreement include?

The scope of the work, how fees are shared, how long the agreement lasts, how it can be ended and who is responsible for what. Read it carefully, and ask questions before signing.
