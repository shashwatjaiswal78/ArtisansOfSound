What Does an Artist Management Company Do? The Work Behind a Show

21 October 2026 | 2 min read

Planning an event? We bring you the right act and handle the contract and logistics.

Get a quote on WhatsApp

When a performance goes smoothly, the audience sees the artist and the music. What they do not see is the work that made it possible. This is what an artist management company does behind the scenes.

Finding and filtering opportunities

A manager receives enquiries, decides which ones suit the artist, and says no to those that do not. That includes checking the venue, the crowd, the date and whether the budget is realistic.

Negotiating fees and terms

Artists often find it uncomfortable to negotiate their own fees. A manager handles that conversation professionally, agreeing the price, the duration, the travel and the technical needs.

Contracts and payments

Every booking should be backed by a written contract that covers what was agreed. The manager makes sure the advance is received, the contract is signed and the balance is paid on time.

Logistics

  • Travel, accommodation, food and local transport for the artist and the team.
  • Sound, instruments and technical requirements, shared with the venue in advance.
  • Timings, soundcheck and the schedule on the day.

Being the single point of contact

Clients and venues deal with the management team, not the artist directly. That keeps communication clear and gives the artist the space to prepare.

Building the artist's career

Beyond individual shows, a good manager helps the artist grow, with better video material, a stronger reputation and more of the right kind of work over time.

Signs of a good management company

  • It tells you the truth about an act, including when it is not the right fit.
  • Every quote is itemised and every booking has a written contract.
  • Artists are paid fairly and on time.
  • You have one clear point of contact before and on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What does an artist manager do day to day?

Answers enquiries, negotiates terms, manages the calendar, coordinates travel and technical needs, follows up on payments and plans the artist's growth.

Do all artists need a manager?

Not at the very start, but as bookings grow, handling them alone takes time away from the music. A manager gives the artist time and a professional front.

How are managers paid?

It varies between companies and agreements, and some work on commission or an agreed fee. Ask for the terms in writing before signing.

What is a rider?

A rider lists an artist's technical and hospitality requirements, such as sound equipment, stage needs, travel and meals. It is shared with the client and the venue in advance.

What should be in an artist contract?

The date, timings, fee, payment schedule, technical needs, travel and stay, cancellation terms and who is responsible for what.
